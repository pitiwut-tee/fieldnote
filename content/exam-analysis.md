Style (matters more than topic):
1. Almost every item hangs on a figure, graph, table or described experiment; very few bare recall items.
2. Many items are "which statements are correct" with 4–5 statements mixing calculation and concept.
3. Each paper has several UNSEEN items: a short passage introduces something new (a rocket payload with a v–t graph,
   terminal velocity with f = kv, a thermal-expansion formula to design a thermometer, a calibration line for
   concentration, insect hormones, buzz pollination, a pseudo-ruminant) and asks the student to reason from it.
4. Experiment logic is tested inside content items: control group, which variable to change, what a result supports.
5. Calculations chain 2–3 steps and cross topics (density with buoyancy, concentration with heat, power with heat).
6. Everyday or Thai contexts (old steelyard balance, gold shop, hot springs, a province's climate, maglev).

What this means for the bank:
- Biggest gaps: human body (s9), plants (s8), motion and energy (s16), earth (s20). Solutions (s4) is already deep.
- Every new science set should include figure/table/experiment items and at least one "which statements are correct".
- Build short unseen-passage sets in physics and biology (we only have chemistry-flavoured ones).
- Add item type "pick 2" as a minority format only.

What is new compared with 2565 + 2567:
1. Topics marked "not seen" before DID appear: circuits (2, incl. the last item of the paper), lenses/dispersion (1),
   genetic crosses (2), cell division (1), chemical vs physical change (2). Over three papers no science area is safe to skip.
   s5 and s17 are ม.3 term-2 content (school-plan.md: not taught at school before the exam) and still appeared twice each:
   this confirms the plan to teach them ourselves during ม.3 term 1.
2. Thermal expansion has now appeared in all three papers (2565 formula/thermometer, 2567, 2569 graph) and liquid
   pressure/simple machines keep returning: steady topics.
3. Item formats: 4 options on most items, 5 options on some (bridge circuit, lens diagrams, the chemistry grid). A new
   format: 7 statements ก–ช and the options are whole ✓/✗ patterns, so every statement must be judged (no "pick the one
   true line" shortcut). Several items have pictures or graphs AS options (dam forces, energy-vs-time graphs, ray diagrams).
4. "Judgment" items: choose the most sensible action or decide whether a friend's claim is right (genetic counselling,
   when to see a doctor, whether a control is needed). The right option names a reason, not just a fact.
5. Higher-school physics in disguise, but every item has a ม.ต้น route (the tutor shows "method 1 with ม.ต้น knowledge":
   equal potential → no current; work = change in KE; vectors drawn as arrows). We teach the ม.ต้น route, never the formula
   shortcut alone.

Trap types seen (for distractors and `optErr`):
- mass as a distractor when the work done is equal (options "3 times", "√3 times");
- velocity/speed and displacement/distance after part of a circle (same speed, different velocity; equal displacement
  magnitude at two different times);
- ideal voltmeter = no current through it; swapping two series parts changes nothing; raising one R lowers the current;
- balanced bridge: the middle part can be removed;
- force from liquid pressure drawn horizontal or vertical instead of perpendicular to the wall;
- graph slope in mm per °C used as m per °C (answer off by a power of ten);
- choosing the wrong arm of a lever as the effort arm;
- percentages in genetics read as "certain" or "safe" (25% means each pregnancy; 75% unaffected still includes carriers);
- a ratio like 298:102 not recognised as about 3:1;
- a correlation stretched too far ("same latitude so same rain");
- an experiment without a control, or two trials that differ in more than one thing.

Format targets for new science sets: some 5-option items; at least one item per set whose options are pictures or graphs;
one ✓/✗-grid item per mixed set; one judgment item ("most sensible action" / "is the friend right, and why").
Untaught spells (s5, s10, s17, s18, s20 at the time of writing) go into future lessons and their own sets, never into the
old-topic sweep or a checkpoint until taught (hard rule 6 and the checkpoint rule "taught ≥ 7 days earlier").

Notes:
- Cone and pyramid volume DID appear (2567, 2568) although they are ม.3 content — keep m19.7 in scope.
- Statistics items are not "compute the mean": they give conditions and ask for an unknown value or the smallest n.
- Unseen sets teach a definition/formula in ~1 page, then ask 5 graded items; the last ones need real insight.
- Number theory is the single biggest taught-content group and our bank has only 4 items there.
- Not seen: quadratic equations, inequalities, trigonometry, circle theorems, transformations, graphs of linear functions
  (taxicab unseen used coordinates and a line). Weak evidence, same caveat as science.

What this means for the bank:
1. Build number theory (m5) and patterns (m6) well beyond textbook level; add a "counting & logic" strand (not in the map yet).
2. Geometry: composite-area and area-ratio problems, volumes incl. cone/pyramid, Pythagoras inside other figures.
3. Create math unseen-passage sets (own writing): number bases, binary/ASCII-like codes, new distance or operation definitions.
4. Use more written-answer (fill-in) items at level 3; keep statistics but shift to "condition" problems.
