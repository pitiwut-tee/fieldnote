By area: biology ≈ 22 (37%), chemistry/matter ≈ 18 (30%), physics ≈ 15 (25%), earth/atmosphere ≈ 5 (8%).
Not seen in either recalled paper: acids–bases, chemical equations, Punnett squares, light/waves, astronomy, circuits.
(Recalled papers are incomplete, so "not seen" is weak evidence — lower priority, not zero.)

Style (matters more than topic):
1. Almost every item hangs on a figure, graph, table or described experiment; very few bare recall items.
2. Many items are "which statements are correct" with 4–5 statements mixing calculation and concept.
3. Each paper has several UNSEEN items: a short passage introduces something new (a rocket payload with a v–t graph,
   terminal velocity with f = kv, a thermal-expansion formula to design a thermometer, a calibration line for
   concentration, insect hormones, buzz pollination, a pseudo-ruminant) and asks the student to reason from it.
4. Experiment logic is tested inside content items: control group, which variable to change, what a result supports.
5. Calculations chain 2–3 steps and cross topics (density with buoyancy, concentration with heat, power with heat).
6. Everyday or Thai contexts (old steelyard balance, gold shop, hot springs, a province's climate, maglev).

What this means for the bank:
- Biggest gaps: human body (s9), plants (s8), motion and energy (s16), earth (s20). Solutions (s4) is already deep.
- Every new science set should include figure/table/experiment items and at least one "which statements are correct".
- Build short unseen-passage sets in physics and biology (we only have chemistry-flavoured ones).
- Add item type "pick 2" as a minority format only.

## Mathematics

Format (90 minutes; changes a little each year):
- sat Nov 2566: 33 items — 16 multiple choice (5 choices; 2.5 or 4 pts) + 12 written-answer incl. 5 unseen (3 pts each).
- sat Nov 2567: 27 items — 12 multiple choice (4 choices) + 10 written-answer + 5 unseen.
- sat 16 Nov 2568: 27 items, 90 pts — 12 multiple choice (2.5 pts) + 15 written-answer (3–5 pts) incl. 5 unseen.
More than half of the points come from written answers (no choices to eliminate).

Notes:
- Cone and pyramid volume DID appear (2567, 2568) although they are ม.3 content — keep m19.7 in scope.
- Statistics items are not "compute the mean": they give conditions and ask for an unknown value or the smallest n.
- Unseen sets teach a definition/formula in ~1 page, then ask 5 graded items; the last ones need real insight.
- Number theory is the single biggest taught-content group and our bank has only 4 items there.
- Not seen: quadratic equations, inequalities, trigonometry, circle theorems, transformations, graphs of linear functions
  (taxicab unseen used coordinates and a line). Weak evidence, same caveat as science.

What this means for the bank:
1. Build number theory (m5) and patterns (m6) well beyond textbook level; add a "counting & logic" strand (not in the map yet).
2. Geometry: composite-area and area-ratio problems, volumes incl. cone/pyramid, Pythagoras inside other figures.
3. Create math unseen-passage sets (own writing): number bases, binary/ASCII-like codes, new distance or operation definitions.
4. Use more written-answer (fill-in) items at level 3; keep statistics but shift to "condition" problems.
