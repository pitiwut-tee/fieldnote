# Exam patterns (summaries only, no copied questions)

Source documents stay on the father's computer. This file summarises their *style* so new questions match the
real exams. Never copy a question, its numbers or its figure; write a new situation that trains the same skill.

## O-NET ม.3 คณิตศาสตร์ (2566 paper read in full; 2564–2568 files to be added)

- 20 items, 90 minutes: 16 multiple choice (4 options) + 4 numeric-entry answers (fill digits, e.g. 15.00, 0.54).
- Almost every multiple-choice item is a **real-life situation of 3–5 lines**, often with a diagram, table or graph:
  shop stock in a given ratio; national budget in scientific notation; garden paving cost from areas with an
  unknown x; a parabolic arch with heights; a percentage table with one missing row; an exchange-rate line graph
  ("which statement is surely true"); mean of two kinds of boxes; cutting cards from posters (minimum sheets);
  cylinder hole in a concrete block (volume → radius); pyramid tent volume → height; distances on a map via
  Pythagoras; arc length of a rotating clock hand; ladder against a wall with a 30° angle; similar triangles for
  an area; median and mean with a missing value; probability of drawing letters.
- Skills are ม.1–3 core, but **2–3 steps each**: set up from words → compute → choose. Pure "plug into a formula"
  items are rare.
- Numeric-entry items are bare algebra/number work: simplify a radical/exponent expression; a "two numbers" word
  puzzle; right triangle with √3 lengths; mean/median/range → find the smallest value.
- Distractors are the results of typical slips (forgetting a unit conversion, using the wrong part of a ratio,
  stopping one step early).

### What this means for our questions
- Level-3 questions should look like this: a short real situation + a figure/table (`fig`), 2–3 steps, options
  built from real slips (`optErr`). About half of all practice questions in a math set should be situational.
- Keep a numeric-entry (`num`) challenge in every math lesson, with an exact answer.
- Monthly mixed sets should include one graph-reading "which is surely true" item and one table-with-missing-row
  item.
- O-NET is the **floor**. The science-school first round is harder (longer reading, multi-concept, less
  routine); see `school-plan.md` for that exam's weighting. Once a topic is "ชำนาญ", its probes should be at least
  O-NET level-3 difficulty.
